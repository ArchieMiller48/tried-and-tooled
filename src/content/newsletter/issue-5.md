<h2>Featured stack: analyse a spreadsheet</h2>

<p><strong>The job:</strong> you have a small export of orders or sales and want the headline numbers and a few charts, without building a pivot table.</p>

<div class="callout">
<ol>
<li><p><strong>Write the question first:</strong> name the column you care about and what a wrong answer would look like.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Your own numbers:</strong> paste rows into Claude Free (code execution is listed on Free) and read the result against the sheet.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Web-sourced cells:</strong> Fill with Gemini in Google Sheets on Google AI Pro shows the web source in each cell. Open the source before you act on a cell.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Want a quick dashboard?</strong> Datastripes' guest mode is worth a look (our note is below).</p></li>
</ol>
</div>

<ul>
<li><p><strong>Top pick: Google Sheets on Google AI Pro</strong>, $19.99/mo monthly in the US (checked 9 Nov). <strong>Budget pick: Claude Free</strong>, $0. Pro is $20/mo (checked 9 Nov). <strong>Avoid for this job: Google AI Plus.</strong> On 9 Nov its plan card did not list Sheets.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>What we actually saw with Datastripes (7 Nov):</strong> signed out, with no account and no card. We uploaded a 20-row bakery CSV and got a story view in about 3.3 seconds. The auto dashboard said Total units 102, Avg unit price 3.55 and 3 products, and we checked all three against the CSV ourselves. It showed a € sign on a GBP column, and it had no time-trend chart. PDF and PPTX export need a signup. Listed prices: Instant €0, Pro €19/mo. We haven't tested Google Sheets or Claude here yet, so the page stays marked <em>pending hands-on test</em>.</p></li>
</ul>

<h2>One practical tip</h2>

<p><strong>When an AI dashboard says "average", check which average it means.</strong> In our Datastripes test, "Avg unit price 3.55" was the simple mean of the price column. Weighted by units sold, the average was about 3.13. Both numbers are correct, but they answer different questions. If you're pricing or forecasting, you probably want the weighted one, so do that one sum yourself before you quote the figure.</p>
