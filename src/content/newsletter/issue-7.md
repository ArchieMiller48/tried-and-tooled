<h2>Featured stack: check if a piece of writing is AI</h2>

<p><strong>The job:</strong> a tutor, client or editor has asked whether a draft looks generated, and you want an answer before you paste in something long.</p>

<div class="callout">
<ol>
<li><p><strong>The draft:</strong> take one section and write down the word count, so you know whether it fits a free cap.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>The check:</strong> run it through Grammarly Pro, or GPTZero Free if you're inside 10,000 words a month.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>The reading:</strong> treat the score as a prompt to look again, not proof that a person did or didn't write it.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>The edit:</strong> if the sentences still need work, that's a different job. Use the <a href="https://archiemiller48.github.io/tried-and-tooled/use-cases/proofread-and-edit/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">proofread page</a>.</p></li>
</ol>
</div>

<ul>
<li><p><strong>Top pick: Grammarly Pro</strong>, which lists AI-text detection on Pro. The visible card says try for $0.00; plan data lists $30 for 1 month, $60 for 3 months or $144 for a year (checked 23 Nov 2026, <a href="https://www.grammarly.com/plans" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">plans</a>). <strong>Budget pick: GPTZero Free</strong>, up to 10,000 words a month; paid dollar amounts weren't in the page HTML (checked 23 Nov 2026, <a href="https://gptzero.me/pricing" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">pricing</a>). <strong>Avoid for this job: Grammarly Free.</strong> AI-text detection is a Pro addition, not part of Free.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>What we actually saw with GPTZero Free (24 Nov):</strong> signed out, no account, three texts on the free scan (GPTZero Model 5.0b). A 2018 Wikipedia passage scored 100% human, an AI-written text scored 100% AI, and a mix of human and AI paragraphs was called a mix with only the AI paragraph highlighted. That was one human sample only, so we haven't tested false positives on more human writing, and Premium, plagiarism and file upload weren't part of it. Grammarly Pro and Grammarly Free weren't tested for this job, so the page stays marked <em>pending hands-on test</em>.</p></li>
</ul>

<h2>One practical tip</h2>

<p><strong>Before you trust any checker, feed it something you already know the answer to.</strong> This week GPTZero Free got our three known texts right: a human Wikipedia passage, an AI-written one and a mix. Scam Link Check flagged Google's test phishing link but said "No known risk found" for a fake Royal Mail text whose domain swapped an l for a capital I. One known-good and one known-bad sample takes two minutes and tells you how far to trust a green light. Treat "nothing found" as weak reassurance, not an all-clear.</p>
