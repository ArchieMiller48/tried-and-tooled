<h2>Featured stack: research and summarise sources</h2>

<p><strong>The job:</strong> read a pile of sources and say what they found, without a single made-up citation.</p>

<div class="callout">
<ol>
<li><p><strong>Question:</strong> write one sentence naming the question, the kind of source you'll accept and what the summary has to answer.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Find papers:</strong> Consensus. Open the paper behind any claim before you keep it.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Summarise what you have:</strong> Elicit Basic for papers in its index, or Gemini Notebook for files you already downloaded.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Write it up:</strong> your own doc, with quotes and links kept next to the summary.</p></li>
</ol>
</div>

<ul>
<li><p><strong>Top pick: Consensus Pro.</strong> $20/mo, or $144/year, with unlimited Pro messages and 15 Deep reviews a month (checked 26 Oct). <a href="https://help.consensus.app/en/articles/10087865-subscription-plans" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source</a></p></li>
<li><p><strong>Budget pick: Elicit Basic.</strong> Free, with unlimited search across more than 138 million papers, unlimited summaries and unlimited chat with full-text papers. Its research agent and reports are limited (checked 26 Oct). <a href="https://elicit.com/pricing" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source</a></p></li>
<li><p><strong>Avoid for a long review: Consensus Free.</strong> 10 Pro messages and 3 Deep reviews a month is a taster, not a literature review. Paper search on Free is unlimited, so use it to find papers and do the reading elsewhere. <a href="https://help.consensus.app/en/articles/10087865-subscription-plans" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source</a></p></li>
</ul>

<p><strong>What we actually saw (26 Oct):</strong> on a free Consensus account, a test question returned a cited answer and a Consensus Meter in about 25 seconds, and a full report in about 60 seconds. That's one quick check, not our full test. Elicit wasn't part of it, and the standard task (same question and five PDFs in each tool, scored on claims that trace back to a real passage) is still to come, so the page stays marked <em>pending hands-on test</em>.</p>

<h2>One practical tip</h2>

<p><strong>On a credit-priced tool, check what happens at zero before you rely on it.</strong> Greptile's Pro plan is now prepaid credits, and its docs say reviews pause when the balance hits zero. CodeRabbit charges per seat instead, with an hourly cap on reviews per developer. Neither is better for everyone: a credit plan suits a few big pull requests a week, a seat plan suits a steady stream. Our <a href="https://archiemiller48.github.io/tried-and-tooled/compare/coderabbit-vs-greptile/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">CodeRabbit vs Greptile comparison</a> puts the two side by side with prices checked 27 Oct.</p>
